Notification preferences

Twelve categories, three channels, three layers of setting stacked on top of each other — and one rule that decides the whole design: mandated means cannot mute, never must-SMS. Silent force and silent suppression are both prohibited, so every cell in this grid says what will actually happen and, where it differs from what the person chose, why.

The hard part is not the matrix, it is the honesty. A locked row has to explain that it is locked against muting and not against choosing a channel. An SMS column has to say why it is unavailable rather than sitting greyed and mute. And when a role default changes underneath somebody's own choice, the admin's affected-count has to exclude them and say it did.

Rows use Section J's per-key anatomy where a preference is a key. Components are B1-DS verbatim; the shell is B6-S11's.

v2 — scope-limited reopen, 3 Aug 2026. Adds the shared ◼ stop-controls button to the top bar, giving the incident page its second route and meeting Doc 16 AC-10 structurally. Nothing else on this screen changed.

Delivered: K01 (all twelve registered categories against in-app, email and staff-SMS — §A both themes), K02 (SMS offered where there is a phone and an opt-in, and explaining itself where there is not — §A dark, §C dark), K03 (the three layers plus mandated, each with glyph and word, with the key printed on the screen — §A light), K04 (effective result with its four-line why — §A light cards, §B light), K05 (role-defaults admin view — §B light), K06 (mandated rows naming who mandated them, on which single channel, and why — in-app for the supervisor in §A light and §C light, SMS for the agent in §A dark and §C dark), K07 (override beats role default, with the affected-count excluding those seven people and saying so — §B light), K08 (SMS-mandated with no phone falling back to email in channel-priority order — genuinely, because the agent has muted in-app and email herself — with the “cannot mute, not must-SMS” statement beside it, §A dark and §C dark), K09 (quick-mute writing to this model, computed in the user's own timezone — §B dark, §A both themes, §C both themes), K10 (grid accessibility, with the three announcements written out — §B dark), K11 (loading, empty and save failure — §B both themes).

Deviations to record: quick-mute stays operable at 375 while the grid is read-only. It is a bell action that happens to write here, and the bell is on the phone; a read-only mute button would be a button that does nothing.

One thing I want your eye on: every cell spells out its layer in words — “Role · on” rather than a half-filled dot. It makes the grid wordier than the usual three-state matrix, and at twelve rows by three columns that is thirty-six small labels. I think it is right, because the layer is the whole point of the feature and a dot is exactly the kind of thing an operator learns wrong once and then trusts. Worth your call before it goes to i18n — it is thirty-six more strings per language.
