Report page anatomy

Doc 15 FR-07 fixes one anatomy for all eight families: header KPI row, primary chart, breakdown table. Designed once here, then applied eight times — so every decision on this page is a decision made eight times over.

Two obligations shape it. Doc 15 FR-03: every number discloses its formula, exclusions and calendar. Doc 15 FR-08 and BR-06: every number is reachable — no aggregate ships without a way into the records behind it, and a rate drills into its numerator and denominator separately. The worked example is the Agent report, because it also exercises the permission gate.

§B

Disclosure, dates and calendars

F03 · F04 · F06 · F07 · F08 · F09

Doc 15's stated goal is that no two screens ever show two different numbers for the same question. The formula disclosure is how that goal becomes visible: when two people disagree about a figure, they open the same panel and find out they were asking different questions. It is a focusable button, not a hover-only tooltip.

F08 · Two calendars inside one comparison
◷ These two periods used different business hours
July ran on 08:00–22:00 seven days. June ran on 08:00–20:00 weekdays only. The comparison is still valid, but part of the improvement is the longer day, not faster work.

Doc 15 EC-09 requires both calendars be named. The interpretive sentence is an accepted addition that renders only against a recorded change — so the banner has two states, and both are drawn here.

Owner decision — the interpretive sentence renders conditionally. It appears only when the compared window spans a recorded business-hours change, and is phrased from that record rather than inferred — the platform knows the old hours, the new hours and the date they changed, so it says exactly that. Where no such record exists the banner names both calendars and stops, as Doc 15 EC-09 requires.
F08 · the untriggered state — no recorded change
◷ These two periods used different business hours
July ran on the Payments calendar. June ran on the tenant default. The comparison is still valid.

The two calendars differ, but no change record explains why — the queue was simply moved onto a different calendar at some point outside this window. With nothing to phrase the sentence from, the banner names both and stops rather than guessing at the cause.

§D

States, 375 and print

F10 · F17 · F18 · F19

At 375 the anatomy survives but the chart cannot: a 31-point line at 343px wide is a smudge. So the primary chart becomes a headline plus a sparkline plus the table it was drawn from — the same information, honestly re-shaped, rather than the same picture shrunk past legibility.

Light
F10 · An empty range is not a zero
No data in this range
There were no tickets in Verification between 02:00 and 05:00. That is an absence of events, not a score of zero.

A flat line at zero would be read as terrible performance. The difference matters enough that Doc 15 §8 names it explicitly.

F19 · Loading, and the lag banner
Figures may be up to 20 minutes behind. We are catching up on yesterday's records. Today's numbers will keep changing until this clears.

Doc 15 FS-02 · NFR-03. Serving a stale number without saying so is the failure the whole verified-badge apparatus exists to prevent, so the banner is loud and sits above the numbers, not below them.

Delivery note

Delivered: F01–F19, both themes, 1440 and 375. The anatomy, the global and family-specific filter bars, date semantics with automatic grain, comparison deltas, the formula disclosure with its business-hours switch, the two-calendar warning, filter persistence, the empty range, the drill-down slide-over with rate-halves and the 10k cap, scope duality, the elided-agent variant, export-this-list, the mobile filter sheet, and print.

Owner decision — the interpretive sentence, accepted and gated. Doc 15 EC-09 requires both calendars be named; the interpretive reading is an accepted addition that renders only when the compared window spans a recorded business-hours change, phrased from that record rather than inferred. Both states are drawn in §B — triggered, and the no-recorded-change case where the banner names both calendars and stops.

Judgement call: at 375 the primary chart becomes headline + sparkline + a link into the daily figures, rather than a shrunken 31-point line. Doc 15 §9 asks for exactly this ("charts simplify to sparkline plus table"), so it is compliant — but it is the one place the phone shows a genuinely different artefact from the desktop, and I would rather you saw that stated than discovered it.
