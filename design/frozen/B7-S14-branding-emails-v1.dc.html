Branding — Emails

The chrome around every message the platform sends, and the group where an operator's edit reaches somebody's inbox rather than their screen. The bodies themselves shipped in Section I; what is edited here is the frame they sit in — logo band, address block, legal line, copyright — per language, once, for all of them.

Two constraints are made visible rather than left to the renderer. The safe HTML subset is stated at the editor, with link targets rendered in the preview, because a branded email is the easiest phishing surface an operator could accidentally build. And the reset template is byte-invariant to account existence — one template, no variants, nothing in it that could differ depending on whether the address belongs to anyone.

Built on B1-DS v4. Editing is desktop per Doc 16 §9; 375 is the read-only rendering.

§A

Chrome, templates, and the preview that goes to your own inbox

N01 · N02 · N03 · N04 · Doc 16 FR-01, T-02, SEC-02, §8

Eight templates share one frame, so the list shows which of them a chrome change touches before it is made — and send-to-self exists because no rendered preview in a browser tells you what Outlook will do with it. The address block is a legal requirement in most jurisdictions the operator sends into, which is why it is a field here and not an afterthought in a footer string.

§B

Rolling one template back, and the part that always sends

N05 · N06 · Doc 16 FS-02, T-02

Email templates version inside the theme's email group, so a template that renders badly in one client rolls back alone rather than taking the whole brand with it. The restore point is named — which version, published when, by whom — because “roll back” without a named destination is a leap in the dark at the exact moment nobody wants one.
