§15 answered — what the three edge cases changed

Two rows are new and one is sharper. EC-10 confirmed staff-set is sticky and added something I would not have invented — a detected language offers itself once per change as a chip rather than nagging or overwriting, which is now written into H03. EC-11 is new: concurrent edits to one field are last-writer-wins with a conflict toast, so the agent who lost sees it happen instead of discovering it later — row A16. EC-12 is new and the most consequential: a merge whose combined open linked work would break Doc 06's cap of three is refused at the confirm step with the offending tickets named — row D13. That one is a merge I would otherwise have drawn as always-possible.

What I have deliberately left out

  • Doc 08 §3 puts conversation mechanics (Doc 04), RG detection and its queue routing (Docs 09, 11), CSAT capture (Doc 12), the erasure approval workflow (Doc 17) and marketing consent out of scope. Where this batch renders one of them — the RG banner, a satisfaction rollup, an export initiation — the row is here; the machinery behind it is not mine.
  • The resolver (FR-07, FR-08) is a backend contract with no interface of its own. It appears only where an agent can see its consequences: shell profiles, the duplicate-phone block, and the deliberate absence of any auto-linking affordance.
  • Acceptance criteria AC-01 to AC-09 and the technical rows T-01 to T-08 are build obligations, not screens, and are not checklist rows.
  • Doc 08 §19's future expansion — self-service portal, household graph, enrichment connectors, ML duplicate suggestions — is explicitly out of v1 and absent here. So is any identity confirmation flow: BR-01 forbids a verified affordance existing anywhere, and AC-09 makes that a repo-wide test.
