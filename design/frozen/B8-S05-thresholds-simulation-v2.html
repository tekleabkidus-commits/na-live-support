Thresholds & simulation

B8-S05-thresholds-simulation-v2 · Section E · checklist rows E01–E07 (+ X5 enforced) · Doc 09 FR-11, BR-07, T-06, NFR-07, EC-07, US-06 · §8 §9 §13

Four numbers decide who talks to a machine and who talks to a person. The design's law is BR-07, made physical: you cannot publish a threshold you haven't watched play out. Move a slider and the simulation re-runs yesterday against your pending values — real counts, real deltas, and the actual conversations that would have flipped. Publish stays locked until the simulation is fresh for exactly the values you're about to publish; touch a slider again and it re-locks.

The RG threshold lives under the same roof but answers to a harder judge: its row carries the measured precision readout against the labeled fixture set, because a false accusation is the costlier error (NFR-07). Backoffice shell is B6-S11 law — context only. Every number on this page also exists as text (§13); nothing is bar-only.

§I — Contract (build notes on the frame)
X5 audited · §13 · AC-03 · T-06
Contract
· X5 audited, no exceptions: Publish is disabled unless the last simulation matches the pending values exactly; any slider change re-locks it. A no-history tenant has nothing to publish — the fallback chain serves platform defaults — so no state of this screen, anywhere, renders an enabled Publish without a fresh simulation.
· Simulation numbers are the engine's own replay (AC-03 parity: what the panel predicts is what the engine would do) — the UI never estimates.
· Sliders are keyboard-adjustable with the numeric value announced; every bar has its numbers beside it; the full result exists as a text table (§13).
· The RG row always shows the precision readout next to any pending change (T-06) — you cannot move rg_min without seeing what it does to precision.
· Flip samples are anonymized on this surface; the transcript behind a sample re-applies real ticket permissions.
· Deltas use arrows AND words ("+12% more"), colored second, worded first.
