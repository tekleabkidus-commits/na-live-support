The branding editor — shell

The Doc 16 half of the batch starts here. Three panes: what you are editing on the left, the fields in the middle, and what it will look like on the right — the preview changes with the group, so a colour change and an email header are never judged from the same picture.

One rule governs the whole surface and it is counter-intuitive enough to be stated on screen: the editor is never themed by the draft. It stays in the platform's own colours while only the preview renders the tenant's. That is what makes a broken draft recoverable — you can always read the tool that fixes it.

Editing is desktop, per Doc 16 §9 and your 3 August ruling; 375 ships as the read-only rendering behind the documented notice. Tenant is Selam Bet throughout.

What the Amharic actually did to this layout: nothing that required a change. Nothing was forced, and that is the finding. The Amharic soft-lock sentence runs about 48% longer than the English — 1084 px against 731 px — and still fits one line; the Amharic banner is 57 px tall against English’s 61 px, because top-aligning the glyph removes the centring slack. So all three property changes on that banner (align-items: flex-start, flex-wrap: wrap, min-width: 300px) are insurance that does not engage at 1440 — the same status as the header’s flex-wrap, and it would be special pleading to call one forced and the other insurance. Also not real: the rail does not wrap (all seven labels fit one line, measured), and the header does not wrap either — its three children sum to 437 px inside 1242 px, so flex-wrap is present as insurance against a longer tenant name and never engages here. The 2 px group gap stays on its own merit: at 1 px the wrapped-label insurance would read as two items if it ever did engage, and 2 px costs nothing.
