Settings Backoffice — shell & the per-key contract

Fourteen groups and one row. That is the whole design: every key in the product renders as the same six-part row — label, description, typed control, validation, provenance, reset — so that a key registered next quarter appears in its group looking exactly right with nobody drawing it. The IA is data, not layout, and this screen is the proof.

Two things the contract buys that are worth naming. A consequence is shown before the save, not after — the timezone key is the worked example, and it is the difference between an admin who understands what they just did to every SLA clock and one who finds out tomorrow. And a locked floor looks locked: a platform minimum uses the same lock as a mandated notification row, because it means the same thing — you may go higher, never lower.

Branding is in the nav as a cross-link only; the editor is Batch 7 by your ruling. Components are B1-DS verbatim.

One thing I want your eye on: the timezone consequence quantifies itself — “1,204 open tickets shift by +1 hour, 18 move from due today to breached”, for a real offset change from UTC+3 to UTC+4. I think the number is what makes an admin stop, and the sentence is what makes them click through; worth confirming with your engineers before it becomes a promise.
