B5-S03-tv-mode-v1 checklist D01–D06

TV mode

Doc 15 FR-12 makes this a mode of a dashboard rather than a screen of its own, and SEC-04 makes it a security surface: a wall in a corridor is read by people who never logged in. So two things drive the design — legibility at four metres, and the total absence of anything privileged or interactive.

The wall render is 1920 × 1080 because that is the hardware, not a viewport a person browses. The config panel is the screen a supervisor actually operates, and that ships at 1440 and 375 as the program requires — now fully executable at 375 per your v3 ruling.

§A

The wall — 1920 × 1080, dark

D01 · D02 · D03 · D04

Dark is the right default for an operations room — a bright wall washes the room and tires people over a shift. Type is set for four metres: KPI values at 108px, labels at 30px, table rows at 30px. Nothing here is below 24px, and nothing here can be clicked, because there is no cursor.

§B

The wall — light, and the high-contrast variant

D01 · Doc 15 §9

Light exists for a bright room — a window-facing wall where a dark screen becomes a mirror. High contrast is a separate switch on top of either theme, for a cheap panel or a long viewing distance: it drops the mid-greys entirely, thickens borders to 3px, and pushes every value to pure ink or pure paper. Shown here on light, since that is the harder case.

§D

Config panel — 375, fully executable

D05 · owner ruling, v3

This is the one place the phone is arguably the better device: you are standing in the ops room looking at the wall, and the thing that changes it is in your hand. Reordering uses move buttons rather than drag, which is also the keyboard path. Applying to a live wall confirms, because other people are watching it.
