Draft preview mode

The operator walks their own product wearing the unpublished theme. Nobody else sees it. The whole design problem is a single sentence rendered so unmistakably that nobody ever thinks they are looking at production — and so unobtrusively that they can still read the product underneath it.

The banner is fixed platform chrome the draft cannot reach. If a palette could restyle the thing announcing it, a bad draft could camouflage its own warning — so the banner takes its colours from the mode, never from the theme: near-black on light, near-white on dark, at the same height and the same position under every draft.

This is also the state the hostile-theme sweep snapshots, because a swap under load is where an unthemed corner would show.
R05 · isolation, said out loud

Preview is scoped to the session that started it — not the tenant, not the admin's colleagues, not another tab signed in as someone else. The banner says “visible only to you” rather than “this is a preview” for exactly that reason: the second sentence describes the screen, the first answers the question the operator actually has.

Exiting returns to the published theme immediately and announces the change, so someone using a screen reader is told they have left the draft rather than discovering it later.

The banner is never themed. Its two colourways belong to the mode, not the draft — #22211D on light, #ECEAE4 on dark — at 40 px in the same position under any palette. Forcing one hex across both would put near-black on the #141412 dark shell at about 1.2:1, which is how a warning disappears. The property that matters is that no draft can touch it.

This is the one branding surface that is fully functional on a phone, and the reason is that it changes nothing.
