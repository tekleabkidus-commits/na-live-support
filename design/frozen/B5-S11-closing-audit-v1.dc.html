Closing the two gaps

K08 · L04

§C

Announcements — the reviewable spec

L09 · Doc 15 §13 · Doc 11 §13

A live board is hostile to a screen reader: nine alerts in a minute becomes nine interruptions, and a countdown that announces every second is unusable. Doc 15 §13 asks for polite, batched announcements without saying what the batch or the cadence is — so this panel decides both, in writing, as the thing to review.

What changesWhat is announced, verbatimWhen
Alerts arrivelive feed, S01 §B “3 new alerts. Most urgent: SLA breached, Deposits.”A count plus the highest-severity one — never each alert in turn. Severity order is breach, rescue, at-risk, everything else. aria-live=politeBatched over 5 seconds. A quiet minute announces nothing.
A breach landsSLA, rescue, gateway circuit “SLA breached. Ticket 4812, Deposits, first response missed by 4 minutes.”Breaks the batch and announces alone. This is the one class allowed to interrupt. aria-live=assertiveImmediate, unbatched.
A countdown runstemporary member, reply window “Yonas Tesfaye, temporary in Deposits, 10 minutes left.”The visible number ticks every second; the announcement does not. Silent until the thresholds. aria-live=politeAt 60 min, 10 min, 1 min and expiry only.
A number refreshesKPI tiles, queue depths — nothing —A board that reads its own numbers aloud every ten seconds is unusable. The region is aria-live=off; the values are readable on demand. aria-live=off
Connection degradessocket lost, store rebuilding “Live updates lost. Refreshing every 30 seconds instead.”Announced once on the change, not repeated while it persists. aria-live=politeOnce per state change.
A wizard advancesrestructure, delete “Step 2 of 4. Decide what happens to each reference.”Progress during execution announces per completed chunk, not per ticket — “Batch 9 of 13 complete.” aria-live=politeOn step change; per chunk during execution.
Every threshold above is a decision, not a document quote — Doc 15 §13 asks for polite and batched and stops there. The 5-second batch, the 60/10/1 countdown ladder and the assertive-for-breach exception are mine and are the thing to accept or change.
§D

Reduced motion — four equivalences, as system law

L10 · Doc 11 §9 · Doc 15 §9

Stated once here and inherited everywhere, the way the chart set is. Each equivalence keeps the information and removes only the movement — nothing is hidden under reduced motion, and no state becomes unreadable.

Light
ElementNormallyReduced motion
Live marker LIVEdot pulses LIVEstatic ring, same meaning
Sparkline draws left to right appears complete
Skeleton shimmer sweeps flat, no sweep
Page change TV rotation cross-fades; slide-overs slide in Both cut instantly. Position and focus land identically.
What is not covered by these four
Nothing else in Batch 5 moves. Specifically checked and confirmed static in both modes: delta chips, status pills, progress bars in the wizard and export drawer (they step, they do not animate), the alert-feed age fade (it is opacity by age, not a transition), temporary-member countdowns (the number changes, the chip does not move), and every chart in the approved set — none animates on load.
One deliberate exception: the wizard's per-chunk progress bar continues to advance under reduced motion, because it is the only signal that a four-minute irreversible operation is still running. Doc 11 FR-18 requires visible progress; a frozen bar would read as a hang.
Filed for inheritance
These four equivalences, the exception, and the announcement thresholds in §C are proposed as a B1-DS addendum on approval — the same route as the B12-S01 lightbox chrome. Future batches inherit them rather than re-deciding, and any new animated element states its own equivalence at review.
