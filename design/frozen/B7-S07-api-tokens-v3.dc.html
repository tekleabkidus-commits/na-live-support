API tokens — personal and machine

A token is a credential with a face: someone owns it, it can do a named subset of what that person can do, and it was last used at a particular moment. Design the list around those three and token sprawl becomes visible instead of invisible — which is the whole point, since the risk here is not a dramatic breach but forty forgotten tokens nobody can account for.

The rule that shapes every screen: a token can never exceed its owner's live permissions. Not at creation — at every request. So the scope picker shows what the owner actually holds, and the list says out loud that losing a permission quietly narrows the token within ten seconds.

§B

Creating one — scope, expiry, and the second show-once

G02 · G03 · G04 · Doc 02 FR-18 · Doc 03 §4.1

The scope list is the person's own permissions, not the whole catalog — you cannot grant what you do not hold, and showing keys you could never select would be an invitation to file a support ticket. Keys the owner lacks appear greyed with the reason, so “why can't I pick that?” is answered in place. The token itself then follows the recovery-code contract exactly: shown once, copyable, and the screen says so before it says anything else.

§C

Machine tokens and rotation with no downtime

G06 · G07 · Doc 02 FR-19, BR-03, SS-04

A machine token belongs to a service account with its own role and its own name in the audit trail — never to a person who might leave, and never to “the system”. Rotation is three states rather than a button: create the replacement, run both through an overlap you choose, then revoke the old one. The overlap is the whole point, and the screen counts it down so nobody forgets step three.

§D

The three refusals, the empty list, and the phone

G09 · G10 · Doc 02 FR-20, AC-05, §9

The API's refusals are read by a developer at a terminal, not by an agent — so they are designed as responses, with the missing permission named. That last part is the difference between a ten-minute fix and an afternoon: insufficient_scope that names tickets.reply tells you exactly which checkbox to tick. The docs page in the empty state exists for the same reason.

v2 — what expansion broke, and what I changed

The revoke dialog's button row. Cancel · rotate instead · revoke now fits English on one line; the Amharic runs about a fifth longer and the middle button collided with the danger button. Rather than shortening the Amharic, the row wraps and the destructive button is pushed to the far end by its own margin — so it is never adjacent to the button someone actually wants.

The rotation step list. Step 2 carries a countdown and an instruction; in Amharic at 1.85 line-height it took a third line and pushed step 3 toward the card edge. Row gaps went 10 → 12 px and every step sizes to its content, so the card grows instead of clipping — the correct direction, since the alternative was truncating the one instruction that prevents an outage.

Both are layout corrections, not copy compromises. No Amharic string here was shortened to fit a frame. G and H stay ❌ in the audit until your deferred clearance lines cover them.
