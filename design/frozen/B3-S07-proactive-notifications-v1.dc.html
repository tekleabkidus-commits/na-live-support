Proactive, notifications, multi-tab

Checklist rows H01–H04, I01–I06 · Doc 13 FR-14, FR-17, FR-18, BR-03, BR-06, EC-03, EC-05, EC-09, T-06. The two features most likely to make a player hate a widget: interrupting them, and making noise. Doc 13 constrains both structurally rather than trusting an operator's restraint — one proactive per cooldown, dismissal permanent on that page, permission requested only after the visitor has spoken first, and everything silent while they are already looking at the thread.

H02, H03, H04 · The three guards
Dismissed once, gone for good on this page
Closing the card ends it. Reloading the deposit page, coming back tomorrow, or bouncing between it and the withdrawals page does not bring it back.
One per visitor per cooldown
Default 24 hours, across every trigger the operator has configured — not 24 hours each. Five clever rules cannot add up to five interruptions in an afternoon.
Never over a keyboard
If the visitor is typing into the site's own form — a deposit amount, a phone number — the card waits until the field loses focus. The widget can tell something has focus; it cannot and does not read what is being typed.

No sound, no counter, no notification. Someone watching the conversation does not need to be told it moved — they can see it. This is the rule that decides whether the widget feels attentive or frantic.
I05, I06 · Dark — six tabs, one voice · and the WebView
Six tabs open, one of them in charge
Tab 1 · leader Tab 2 Tab 3 +3
One socket, one sound, one unread count shared across all of them. Close the leader and another takes over within two seconds. Whether the panel is open stays private to each tab — that is a deliberate exception, because a visitor who opened the chat on one tab has not asked for it to pop open on five.
Inside the Android app
The WebView has no Notification API, so that path does nothing at all — no error, no permission prompt, no broken button. Sound defers to the app's own audio focus, so the widget never talks over a video.

Why the switch lives in the menu and not on the thread

A sound toggle on the main surface is a control almost nobody touches taking space from the one everybody does. In the overflow it is two taps away, next to language and the transcript — the three things a visitor changes once, if ever. The operator sets the default; the visitor's choice, once made, outranks it and persists.
