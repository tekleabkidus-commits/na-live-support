Every screen, component and state Doc 11 and Doc 15 require, with its reference. This is the contract the closing audit is checked against, row by row. I have designed nothing yet.

Three things shape this batch. First, it is the batch that introduces charts to the system — Doc 15 §9 sets a chart grammar (one palette per dimension, units from the registry, never colour alone) and I propose that component set for approval before any report uses it. Third, Doc 11 gives me two very different surfaces: a live floor view where a supervisor acts in seconds, and a structure admin where the same person restructures departments carefully, with a wizard that lists everything they are about to break.

Section J is a scope call, not an invention: Doc 11 §8 specifies a full Backoffice → Organization admin, and Batch 6 is fixed to Docs 16, 05 and 03, so this batch is the only place it can land. Cut it here if you meant it elsewhere.

One conflict I will not resolve on my own

Your batch instruction and Doc 15 disagree about phones, so I am showing you both passages rather than picking a winner.

Batch 5 prompt, Step 2: “deliver both viewports together — 1440px desktop AND 375px mobile. Mobile is not a bonus; a weak mobile version is a rejection.”

Doc 15 §9 Responsive: reports phone-readable, “builder ≥ 1024 px (fat-finger policy), live dashboard tablet+ with the Doc 11 touch fallbacks”. Doc 11 §9 says the same of restructuring: “wizards full-screen mobile-capable read-only (execution desktop/tablet per fat-finger policy)”.

So three surfaces — the dashboard builder (H), the live dashboard's drag operations (B/C), and the restructure wizards (J) — are documented as deliberately not phone-executable. My proposal, for your ruling: every row still ships a real 375 design, but for those three the mobile design is read-and-monitor plus dialog-based equivalents of the drag actions, and it says on screen why building and restructuring wait for a bigger surface. That satisfies the touch-fallback clause without pretending a 375 screen can safely execute a 10k-ticket queue migration. Rows B18, C02, H14 and J16 carry this and are marked.

What I have deliberately left out

  • Doc 11 §3 puts SLA policy maths (Doc 12) and supervisor-dashboard rendering (Doc 15) out of its own scope, and Doc 15 §3 puts event production, the SLA clock, AI anomaly generation (Doc 09) and notification transport (Doc 16) out of its. This batch renders their outputs; it does not design their machinery.
  • The Rescue view itself is Doc 07 FR-05 and was Batch 1's. Doc 11 FR-16 only requires that rescue conditions raise alerts here, so rows C12–C13 cover the alert and its deep link, not the view.
  • The monitor/whisper conversation is Doc 07's thread with a supervision banner. Row C08 covers the entry point, the banner and the internal-note typing of a whisper; the thread itself is approved law from Batch 1.
  • Report API (Doc 15 FR-18) has no interface. Technical rows (T-01…T-09 both docs), non-functional budgets and acceptance criteria are build obligations, not screens — except where a document turns one into something visible, like the “verified” badge (NFR-06) or the estimator's banded strings (Doc 11 FR-13), which are rows.
  • Both §19 future-expansion lists — volume forecasting, cohorts, BI connectors, QA scorecards, operator-defined metrics, richer skills, spillover policies, customer-choice routing — are out of v1 and absent here.
