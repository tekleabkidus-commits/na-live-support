§B

The verified badge — and its honest absence

E04 · E05 · Doc 15 NFR-06, FS-01

Doc 15 NFR-06 asks for an integrity indicator with its last-reconciled time. The design problem is that a badge which is always green teaches people to ignore it. So the withheld state is not a smaller version of the same chip — it changes colour, icon, wording and gains a "what this means" line, because it is the state that actually needs reading.

The wording avoids engineering vocabulary entirely: a payments manager should not need to know what a projector or a truth-sync is to understand that yesterday's figures may move.

Delivery note

Delivered: E01–E06, both themes, 1440 and 375. Eight family cards with sparklines from the approved chart set, the AI digest and anomaly feed with range-preserving links, pinned and recent, and the verified badge in all four of its states — verified, withheld after a correction, paused during a rebuild, plus loading, first-run empty and panel failure.

Design decision worth your eye: the withheld badge names the affected family rather than casting doubt on everything. Doc 15 NFR-06 does not specify the granularity; I read a blanket warning as the more damaging default, since it teaches people to distrust reports that are fine. Say the word if you want it blanket instead.

No deviation here: reports were already phone-readable in Doc 15 §9, so this screen predates and is unaffected by the v3 mobile ruling. Next is Section F — the report page anatomy, filters and drill-down, which is the frame all eight families are then built on.
