Restructure wizards

Doc 11 FR-18. Deleting a queue with 1,284 open tickets is the single most destructive action in this product, and the wizard's whole job is to make the consequences legible before they are irreversible — inventory first, a choice per reference, typed confirmation, then per-chunk progress.

The inventory is the part I care most about. A wizard that says "this will affect other things" teaches people to click through. A wizard that says "3 automation rules, 1 SLA policy, 2 saved views and 1,284 tickets point at this queue — here they are" makes the decision real.

This file also carries the 375 renderings for Section J — the admin screens from B5-S09 as well as the wizards. §C covers the queues list, queue detail and the queue-delete wizard; §D covers departments, department detail, teams, priority boosts, both remaining strategy panels, the estimator, deactivate-vs-delete, the inventory failure and the failed save.

▲ Deliberate deviation from Doc 11 §9, on your v3 ruling

Doc 11 §9 restricts wizard execution to desktop or tablet under the fat-finger policy. It is fully executable at 375 here. The typed confirmation is unchanged — the gate is the same, only laid out for a thumb, and every control is 44px or larger.

Delivered: J16–J21, both themes. All four wizard steps at 1440, deactivate-versus-delete with the rule fall-through list, the department delete with the RG replacement requirement blocking progress, the per-system inventory with one source failing, and 375 across Section J: queues list with the empty-department state, queue detail, departments list, department detail with its danger zone, teams with the access warning, the priority-boost list with its locked row, round robin and load based, the estimator bands, deactivate-vs-delete, the incomplete inventory and the failed save, plus the queue-delete wizard at steps 1 and 3. Not drawn at 375: the department-delete wizard (J18) and wizard steps 2 and 4, which reuse the same step chrome shown here — say the word and I will draw them out rather than leave them implied.

Two judgement calls: deactivate is presented above delete rather than beside it, because most people reaching for delete want intake to stop; and the incomplete-inventory state offers "continue with a partial list" rather than blocking outright — Doc 11 §8 asks for retries but does not say whether a partial inventory is a hard stop. Tell me if it should be.
