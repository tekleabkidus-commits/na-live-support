Enforcement, grace, and the admin reset

Policy, made visible. A supervisor whose role now requires two-factor has three days and a countdown; when the days run out the product stops, and the stop has to read as a deadline that arrived rather than an accusation. Then the other half: the single thing an administrator can do about somebody else's two-factor, and the four things they cannot.

Workspace chrome is Doc 07's and Batch 1 law — context only, never rebuilt. Amharic is real text, flagged for native review.

§A

The countdown, and the day it runs out

D01 · D02 · D03 · Doc 02 FR-09

During grace the banner sits under the top bar on every screen, states the days left in words, and offers the way out — it never blocks a queue of waiting conversations. When grace expires the same person meets a full stop instead: enrolment is the only route, the reason is one sentence, and signing out is still allowed because trapping someone in a wizard is not security.

§B

The one thing an admin can do — and the four they cannot

D04 · D05 · D06 · Doc 02 FR-08, BR-02, SEC-07, §4.8

Clearing someone's two-factor is the highest-consequence thing on this page: for a short window that account is protected by a password alone. So it takes a typed name, states the consequence in the present tense, and names the audit event in the dialog rather than in a help centre. The panel beside it is the answer to the question every admin eventually asks — “can I just look at their code?” — placed where they will ask it.

Clear two-factor for Yonas Tesfaye?

Yonas will be asked to set up two-factor again the next time he signs in. Until he finishes, his account is protected by his password alone.

You are not seeing or changing his authenticator — nobody can. This removes the existing setup so he can create a new one, and his recovery codes stop working at the same moment.
Recorded as a high-visibility change. auth.2fa.reset_by_admin — your name, the time, and Yonas's account, visible in the audit trail to anyone who can read it.

What you can do about someone's sign-in

✓Send a password-reset link · he chooses the password, you never see it
✓Unlock the account after too many attempts
✓Clear two-factor so he can enrol again
✓Invite, resend an invite, or disable the account
What nobody can do — including you
✕See or set his password
✕See his two-factor secret or his recovery codes
✕Read a code from his authenticator
✕Sign in as him — this platform has no impersonation, deliberately
To see what he sees, ask for read-only permissions on his queue instead.

Drawing two near-identical screens would have been padding; the frame carries the expired copy, and with grace set to zero the same gate appears on the first sign-in.
