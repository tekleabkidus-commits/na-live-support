Sessions and devices

This table exists to answer one question in about a second: is that me? Everything is arranged around that — the current device pinned at the top and labelled, the rest sorted by how recently they were used, and each row carrying the three facts a person actually matches against: what the device is, where it was, and when it was last active.

The second job is making revocation believable. “Sign out” controls in most products feel advisory. Here the confirmation states what happens and how fast — the session key is deleted and that device's live connection closes within two seconds — because a security control nobody trusts gets used twice and then ignored.

Profile → Security, in the workspace shell — Doc 07's chrome, Batch 1 law, context only. Amharic is real text, flagged for native review.

§C

When a session ends without you asking

F08 · F12 · F13 · Doc 02 §8, EC-02, EC-05

Three ways a session can end mid-sentence, and one rule across all of them: never lose what someone typed. The idle warning arrives two minutes early with a one-click stay. A session revoked elsewhere produces a sign-in modal over the page rather than a redirect away from it. And an account disabled while its owner is working says what happened rather than dissolving into 401s.

Why F12 and F13 look different

F12 is recoverable in five seconds, so it asks for a password and stays on the page. F13 is not recoverable by the person reading it, so it explains, tells them who to ask, and offers no action that would fail. Giving F13 a password field would be cruelty dressed as consistency.
