§A

The chart set — proposed, for approval

rows A01–A16 · Doc 15 §9, §13

One rule, and it is the whole proposal: ink ramp for categories, fixed tokens for states

Six hues are already spoken for by the fixed status set, and B1-DS chose an ink accent precisely so that colour in this workspace only ever means state. A rainbow chart palette would break that on day one. So categories — queues, departments, teams, gateways — are drawn on a six-step ink ramp, and each series also carries a fixed marker glyph, a fixed fill pattern and a direct label. The hue is the weakest of the four encodings, never the load-bearing one, which is what Doc 15 §9 and the batch rule both ask for.

State keeps its documented colour inside charts too: an at-risk bar uses the SLA chip's amber, a breach uses its red, AI-origin series carry the violet accent. Those are not palette choices and are not re-mixed.

A queue keeps its ramp step and its marker for the whole session, on every chart, exactly as Doc 15 §9 requires. The ramp is generated from the accent slot, so a tenant dropping in a brand colour re-skins every chart at once without touching a status hue.

Owner decision — six-series cap. Categorical series cap at six per chart; the rest go to the breakdown table beneath. Six is where the ramp stops being reliably distinguishable. When the cap fires the chart says so, beside the title, so a truncated chart is never mistaken for a complete one.
