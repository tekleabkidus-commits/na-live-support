Degraded & AI-off states

B8-S11-degraded-states-v1 · Section K · checklist rows K01–K08 · Doc 09 FS-01/02/03, EC-11, EC-12, NFR-04, NFR-06, AC-08, AC-09 · §8 — the labeled-degrade pattern, never a silent empty

This screen designs the platform on its bad days — and holds it to one sentence: zero stranded customers, and every degraded surface says what it is. Providers die, quotas run out, indexes corrupt, someone slams the kill switch: in every case the customer's next message still gets a truthful answer (usually "a person is coming, about 4 minutes"), the agent's tools say exactly what still works, and recovery announces itself. Nothing here is an error page; every state is a designed place the product can stand.

The admin's view of these states lives on B8-S08 (provider status); this screen is the same weather as EXPERIENCED — customer, agent, supervisor. Frames reuse the approved S01/S02/S03 anatomies where the state appears inside them.

§H — Contract (build notes on the frame)
K01–K08 · AC-09 · §8 §13
Contract
· Zero stranded customers in every state: the worst case is one message turn to the honest handoff (NFR-04, AC-09's drill).
· Every degraded surface is labeled with what still works, since when, and that recovery is automatic — the labeled-degrade pattern; no state on this screen is a spinner or a bare error.
· Customer-facing frames never carry failure vocabulary — the machinery (provider names, breaker states, refusal text) is staff-side only; EC-11's refusal text in particular never reaches a customer.
· Kill-switch-off and full outage produce the identical customer experience by design; the causes are distinguished only where staff look (S07/S08).
· Recovery never reclaims conversations a human already holds.
· Quota exhaustion finishes the in-flight turn from the 5% reserve before diverting (EC-12); the 80% alert precedes it on the admin side.
